## Problems felt as a driver

Many drivers — including me — run several apps at once. That is already normal. What feels uncomfortable is not multi-app alone, but **anonymous dispatch**: riders do not know who their driver is, and drivers have no direct relationship with riders they have served. Platform economics also often feel like they **take** more than what is transparent to drivers.

As someone who drives myself, I want a model that is **fairer and more honest** — where drivers have identity and contact details riders can reach, not just a dot on the map that disappears after the trip ends.

## What makes Ojekku different

**Ojekku is a platform per driver.** Riders see how to reach the driver — WhatsApp, Telegram, or other channels the driver chooses to publish. Conversations happen on messengers people already use daily; Ojekku does **not** build proprietary in-app chat.

That is intentional: lower early engineering cost, and respect rider/driver habits with WA/Telegram. Ojekku focuses on **showing drivers on the map** and **providing contact** — not locking conversation inside our ecosystem.

## Why Salatiga first

Salatiga has a city size that is **realistic for dogfooding**: small enough for one driver to actually serve, lively enough to test whether riders will contact drivers directly. Starting in a big city with one-person supply is over-promising; in Salatiga we can be honest: **one driver first**, the founder driving while building the product.

Initial services: **ojek** and **taxi**. Cash/offline payment is still accepted — no digital payment required on day one.
